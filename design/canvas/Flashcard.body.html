Flashcard

The only element in the app on radius-card-lg (24px) and shadow-float — it is meant to read as an object you could pick up, not as a panel. Both faces are stacked in one grid cell so the card sizes to the taller one and the pair flips together on a 480ms Y rotation.

Why the back is blue

Cream front, surface-tint back with a primary-border hairline. Mid-flip the surface change is the first thing visible — you know which side you are on before you can read a word of it. The gender chip is repeated on both faces so it never disappears mid-answer.

Two sentences, kept apart

When a word was captured from pasted reading it carries its own source sentence, which sits beneath the dictionary's example behind its own labelled rule. Both are Dutch, both italic — the label is what stops them merging into one block.

One more try

The eyebrow is the card's whole state machine: it asks a question before the flip, names the side after it, and says One more try when a missed word comes back around — so a re-queued card explains itself instead of looking like a bug.

The grade buttons do not exist until the answer is on screen. Both used to be live under an unflipped card next to a question asking whether you knew the word — which invites answering a card you haven't tried to recall, and quietly turns self-assessment into guesswork. Revealing first is what makes the grade mean anything; the card still flips on tap, this is the same gesture given a label.
