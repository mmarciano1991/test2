Word rows & reading

The deck row is the only component that changes shape rather than colour when it opens — the example sentence appears inside the same card, below a rule, instead of pushing out a panel. Two variants exist because two jobs do: the deck row is a control, the compact row is a read-only listing.

Hover swaps the white surface for the canvas cream and cools the border — the row recedes into the page rather than lighting up, which keeps a scrolled list calm.

Deck row — open, with a captured source sentence
dehond dog
Mature
Where you met it
De hond loopt in de tuin.
In context
De hond rent door het park.
The dog runs through the park.

Two sentences, never merged. "Where you met it" is the real sentence the word was captured from; "In context" is the dictionary's own example. Both are Spectral italic, but the labels keep them apart — seeing a word next to where you actually read it is the check a dictionary example alone can't offer.

Three states in one line of prose: untouched, active (soft blue fill, the word currently being looked up) and added (green, underlined). Punctuation and spaces stay plain text, so the paragraph reads as prose rather than as a row of buttons — the negative margins cancel the padding so nothing shifts when a word highlights.
